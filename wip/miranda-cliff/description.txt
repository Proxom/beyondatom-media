Guess before you land: seconds, minutes or hours? 👇

Verona Rupes, on Uranus's small moon Miranda, is the tallest known cliff in the solar system: about 20 km high, roughly ten times the depth of the Grand Canyon.
Miranda is tiny (about 470 km across), so its gravity is weak: 0.079 m/s² at the surface, about 124 times weaker than Earth's (9.82 m/s²), and even weaker at the top of the cliff.
Jump off the top and:
After 5 minutes you have fallen only about 3 km, at about 73 km/h.
After 10 minutes you are still about 7.7 km up.
After 12 minutes 41 seconds you hit the ground at about 194 km/h (54 m/s). Miranda has no air, so nothing slows you down.

NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day gives a rough 12 minutes for this fall; our step-by-step calculation, which includes gravity getting stronger as you get closer to Miranda's center, gives 12 min 41 s.

The math: gravity g = GM / r², with Miranda's mass 6.6 × 10¹⁹ kg (GM ≈ 4.4 km³/s²) and mean radius 235.8 km; we stepped the fall forward in time from rest at 20 km.
The cliff's height is an estimate (older studies gave 5 to 10 km); with 20 km as NASA gives it, the numbers above follow.

About the video: an original animation. The cliff texture, the ground and Uranus are illustrative (Uranus is not to scale), and the astronaut is not to scale. The clock shows the real fall time, sped up about 35 times.
