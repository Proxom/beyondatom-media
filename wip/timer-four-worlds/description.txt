Guess before the end: how long does Pluto's timer take? 👇

Same 30-second sand timer on four worlds. Only gravity changes:
Earth (9.82 m/s²): 30 s
Mars (3.73 m/s²): 49 s
The Moon (1.62 m/s²): 1 min 14 s
Pluto (0.62 m/s²): 1 min 59 s, about 4 times slower than on Earth

Why? Sand pours out of a small hole at a steady rate, and that rate grows with the square root of gravity (the Beverloo law for grains flowing out of a hole). Weaker gravity, slower sand. Pluto's gravity is about 16 times weaker than Earth's (9.82 / 0.62 = 15.8), and √15.8 ≈ 4, so the same timer takes about 4 times longer.

The math: time = 30 s × √(9.82 / g). This assumes the sand flows the same way at low gravity as it does here; experiments in a centrifuge (up to 20 times Earth's gravity) and a low-gravity setup found the same square-root rule.

About the video: an original animation. The timers are drawn the same size on every world; the motion is sped up, and the clocks show the real time. At the end, Earth's and Pluto's timers are flipped and run again side by side at the same speed: when Earth's runs out, Pluto's is only a quarter done. Landscapes are illustrative.
