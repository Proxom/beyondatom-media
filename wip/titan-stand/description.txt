Guess before the end: could you fly on Titan, yes or no? 👇

Titan is Saturn's biggest moon, and the only moon with a thick atmosphere (mostly nitrogen, with about 5% methane).
Air pressure: about 1.5 times Earth's at sea level, so you would not need the bulky pressure suit astronauts wear on spacewalks.
The cold: about -179 °C, and there is no oxygen to breathe. You would need a heavily insulated suit and your own air.
Gravity: only about 1/7 of Earth's. A person who weighs 70 kg on Earth would weigh about as much as 10 kg does on Earth (70 / 7 = 10).
The air near the ground is about 4 times denser than Earth's.
Thick air + weak gravity: Titan is the easiest place in the solar system to fly. Scientists think a person could even strap wings onto their spacesuit, get a running start, flap, and take off. NASA's Dragonfly rotorcraft will use the same thick air and low gravity to fly from place to place on Titan.

About the video: an original animation. The landscape, the astronaut, the wings and the weight scale are illustrations; human flight on Titan has never been tried.
