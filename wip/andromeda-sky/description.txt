TITLE: How Big Is Andromeda in Our Sky? 🌌

Guess first: 1, 3 or 6 full Moons wide? 👇

The Andromeda galaxy (M31) is so close to us that, in angular size, it is about six times the apparent diameter of the full Moon.
From a dark sky site it is easily visible with the unaided eye, but the eye sees only its bright core, as a faint smudge; the full width shows up in long-exposure photos.
It is about 2.5 million light-years away, so the light you see tonight left Andromeda about 2.5 million years ago, long before our species existed (the oldest known Homo sapiens fossils are about 300,000 years old).

About the video: the galaxy is a real NASA image (GALEX, ultraviolet light), brightened and placed next to a drawn full Moon so that the galaxy is 6 Moon widths long, as on the NASA page. To the eye, Andromeda looks much fainter, and only the core is visible. The sky, hills and trees are illustrations.
