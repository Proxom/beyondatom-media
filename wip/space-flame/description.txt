Guess first: how long can a candle burn in orbit? 👇

On Earth, a flame heats the air around it. Hot air is lighter, so it rises and pulls fresh air in from below. That flow stretches the flame into a tall teardrop and carries glowing soot to the tip, which is why it looks yellow.
In orbit everything is in free fall, so hot air does not rise. Fresh oxygen only drifts in slowly, by diffusion. The flame becomes small (about 1.5 cm across), dome-shaped and dim blue.
NASA tested candles on the Space Shuttle and on the Mir space station. On the Shuttle the flames usually lasted under a minute, because the safety box around the candle limited the oxygen. On Mir, some candles burned for up to 45 minutes.
Just before going out, most of the flames pulsed about once a second.
