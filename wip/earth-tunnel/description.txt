Guess before the end: minutes, hours or days? 👇

Imagine a tunnel straight through Earth from the North Pole to the South Pole, with no air inside. Jump in, and gravity alone does the rest:
After 12.7 minutes you pass the bottom of the mantle, 2,891 km down, and enter the core.
After 19.1 minutes you fly through the center at about 9.9 km/s, your top speed.
Past the center, gravity pulls you back toward the middle, so you slow down.
After 38 minutes and 11 seconds you stop, right at the South Pole... and then you fall all the way back.

Why not the "classic" 42 minutes? That number assumes Earth has the same density everywhere. Real Earth is much denser in its core, so gravity stays at or above its surface strength most of the way down (it even rises to about 10.7 m/s² at the top of the core), and the trip is shorter: 38 min 11 s (Klotz, American Journal of Physics, 2015).

The math: we added up Earth's mass layer by layer with the PREM model of Earth's density, worked out gravity at every depth, and stepped the fall forward in time. It gives Earth's mass (5.97 × 10²⁴ kg), surface gravity (9.82 m/s²) and the published 38 min 11 s.

This is a thought experiment: a real tunnel is impossible (heat, pressure), and air would stop you long before the far side. A pole-to-pole tunnel avoids the sideways push from Earth's spin.

About the video: an original animation. The layer sizes are to scale, but the crust and the polar ice are drawn thicker than scale, and the astronaut is drawn far bigger than scale. The clock shows the real fall time; after the far side it runs in slow motion, and the view turns so the South Pole is on top.

Sources:
Alexander R. Klotz, "The Gravity Tunnel in a Non-Uniform Earth", American Journal of Physics 83, 231 (2015) (fall time through the center 38 min 11 s with the PREM model, vs 42 min 12 s for uniform density): https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.1342
A. M. Dziewonski & D. L. Anderson, "Preliminary reference Earth model" (PREM), Physics of the Earth and Planetary Interiors 25, 297 (1981) (density at every depth; core-mantle boundary at 3,480 km from the center, inner core 1,221.5 km): https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-9201(81)90046-7
