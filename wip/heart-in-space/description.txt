TITLE: What Happens to Your Heart in Space? ❤️

Guess first: bigger, smaller or rounder? 👇

Rounder: in a NASA study of 12 astronauts on the International Space Station, the heart became about 9.4% more spherical in weightlessness. Back on Earth it returned to its normal, longer shape shortly after landing.
Why: on Earth, the heart has to pump blood "uphill" from your feet against gravity. In orbit there is no uphill, so the heart does not have to work as hard.
Smaller, too: during his 340 days on the station (2015-2016), astronaut Scott Kelly's left ventricle (the heart's main pumping chamber) lost about 0.74 g of mass per week, even though he exercised six days a week. His heart kept working well.
The surprise: long-distance swimmer Benoit Lecomte, who swam for 159 days in the Pacific Ocean, lost about 0.72 g per week, almost the same rate. Floating in water also takes away much of the work of pumping blood uphill.
