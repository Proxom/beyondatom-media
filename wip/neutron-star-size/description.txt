Guess first: as big as Earth, the Moon, or a city? 👇

A neutron star is heavier than the Sun, yet it is only about 20 km across, about the size of a city.
It is the crushed core left behind when a giant star runs out of fuel, collapses and explodes as a supernova. A typical neutron star has about 1.4 times the Sun's mass; some have up to about twice the Sun's mass.
For scale: the Sun is about 1,391,400 km across, Earth 12,742 km and the Moon 3,475 km.
Neutron-star matter is so dense that one sugar cube of it would weigh about 1 billion tons (1 trillion kg) on Earth, about as much as a mountain.

About the video: an original animation. The "shrink" is a thought experiment that shows the sizes (a real neutron star is not a squeezed Sun). The diameters on the counter are true; the zoom speed is not. The city is drawn for scale and is not a real map. The balance is only an illustration of equal weight.
