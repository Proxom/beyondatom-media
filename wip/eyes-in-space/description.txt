TITLE: What Happens to Your Eyes in Space? 👁️

Guess first: 10%, 40% or 70% of crew members? 👇

Months in orbit can change the shape of your eyes. NASA calls it spaceflight-associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS).
In weightlessness, body fluids shift up toward the head. Scientists believe this headward fluid shift is behind SANS, likely through increased pressure in the head, although the exact cause is still not fully known.
The changes: the back of the eye can flatten, and the optic disc (where the optic nerve enters the eye) can swell.
NASA says signs of SANS appear in roughly 70% of crew members, with big differences from person to person. Two astronauts who noticed their vision change on the space station found that slightly stronger glasses solved the problem.
