Guess before the end: days, weeks or months? 👇

If Earth suddenly stopped moving along its orbit, nothing would hold it up: it would fall straight toward the Sun.
Day 0: Earth stops, 1 AU (about 150 million km) from the Sun.
Day 30: still about 86% of the way out. The Sun barely looks bigger.
Day 41: Earth crosses Venus's orbit (0.72 AU). Sunlight there is about 1.9 times as strong as at Earth.
Day 53: halfway there. The second half takes only about 12 days.
Day 57: Mercury's orbit (0.39 AU). Sunlight there is about 6.7 times as strong.
Day 64.6: Earth reaches the Sun's surface at about 616 km/s.

The math: a straight fall into the Sun is like a squashed orbit whose long axis is 1 AU, so its semi-major axis is 0.5 AU. By Kepler's third law that orbit takes 365.25 × 0.5^1.5 = 129.1 days, and the fall is half of it: 64.6 days. The impact speed comes from energy conservation: v = √(2GM × (1/R − 1/1 AU)), with GM = 132,712 × 10⁶ km³/s² and R = 695,700 km.

This is a thought experiment: Earth can't really stop. The video leaves out the other planets, the Moon and what the heat would do to Earth on the way.

About the video: an original animation. Earth is drawn far bigger than scale. The Sun's size on screen grows as Earth gets closer, but its starting size is exaggerated. The orbit rings are markers, not to scale. The day counter runs at an even pace (about 2.7 days per second).
