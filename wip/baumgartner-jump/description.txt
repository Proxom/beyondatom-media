On 14 October 2012, Felix Baumgartner stepped off a balloon capsule 38,969 m (about 39 km) above Roswell, New Mexico. That is high in the stratosphere, often called "the edge of space" (space itself is usually counted from about 100 km up).

Up there the air pressure is so low that, without his pressure suit, the liquids in his body would start to boil: NASA puts that line at about 63,000 feet (19 km).

34 seconds into the fall he broke the speed of sound, the first person to do it in free fall without a vehicle. 50 seconds in, he hit his top speed: 1,357.6 km/h (Mach 1.25). Then he went into a flat spin for about 13 seconds, fought his way out of it, and opened his parachute after 4 minutes 20 seconds of free fall, about 2.6 km above sea level (about 1.5 km above the ground), and landed safely.

Two years later, on 24 October 2014, Alan Eustace jumped from even higher: 41,422 m, about 2.5 km above Felix's record.
