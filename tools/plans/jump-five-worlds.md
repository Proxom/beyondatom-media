## Description

Same push-off, five worlds. A jump that lifts you 50 cm on Earth would lift you about 1.3 m on Mars, 3.0 m on the Moon, 7.9 m on Pluto and 17.3 m on the dwarf planet Ceres.

With the same take-off speed (about 3.1 m/s), both the height and the time in the air grow as gravity gets weaker: height = v²/(2g) and air time = 2v/g. On Earth you are back down in 0.64 s. On the Moon, with about 1/6 of Earth's gravity, you go 6 times higher and stay up 6 times longer (3.9 s). On Pluto you would hang in the air for about 10 seconds. Ceres's gravity is only about 3% of Earth's, so the same jump would keep you up for about 22 seconds.

About the video: an original computer animation (artist's rendering), shown in real time with all five worlds at the same scale. Simplified physics: the same push-off speed everywhere, no air resistance and no spacesuit (a real suit would make the jump lower), and the spin of each world is ignored. The landscapes are illustrative. Gravity values: NASA surface gravity for Earth (mean 9.82 m/s²), Mars (mean 3.73), the Moon (1.62) and Pluto (mean 0.62); for Ceres, 0.284 m/s², calculated from the mass (GM) and mean radius measured by NASA's Dawn mission.
