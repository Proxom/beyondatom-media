You asked for it, @erwinromadan85 👇 Where is your limit: Neptune, Jupiter or the Sun?

Same 70 kg person, stronger and stronger gravity. On Neptune you would feel only about 81 kg: Neptune has 17 times Earth's mass, but it is so wide that the pull at its cloud tops is just 1.15 g. On Jupiter (2.64 g) you would feel like 185 kg, and at the Sun's surface (28 g) like 1,960 kg.

What stronger gravity does to your body: it pulls your blood down into your legs, so less blood reaches your brain and eyes, and your heart has to beat harder and faster to push it back up. In tests with 1,000 Navy pilots and aviation personnel, relaxed and without g-suits, the average person got grayout at about 4 g, blacked out at about 5 g and passed out at about 5.4 g. Fighter pilots with g-suits and a special straining-and-breathing technique can take about 9 g, but only for seconds. In one test, a 1961 US Navy study, a person stayed at 2 g for 24 hours.

And a neutron star? Its surface gravity is about 100 billion times Earth's. A 70 kg person would weigh as much as 7 billion tonnes do on Earth.

About the video: an original computer animation (illustrative scenery and figure). Jupiter and Neptune have no solid surface: their gravity is given at the cloud tops (the 1 bar level). The Sun's is at its visible surface, and we ignore its 5,500 °C heat. "Feels like" = weight on that world compared with Earth. The blood and vision effects are simplified illustrations; the g limits are averages and vary from person to person.
