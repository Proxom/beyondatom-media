Guess before 0:18: how many seconds do you get after the edge? 👇

This is NASA's own supercomputer simulation of a camera falling into a black hole like the one at the center of our galaxy: 4.3 million times the mass of the Sun, with an edge (the event horizon) about 25 million km across. We flew a vertical camera through NASA's 360° movie and told the story on top.

Your friend, watching from far away, sees your image slow down and seem to freeze just outside the edge. That's why astronomers once called black holes "frozen stars". But you keep falling and cross the event horizon, the point of no return. Inside it, not even light can get back out.

From there you have just 12.8 seconds before spaghettification: the pull on the end of you nearest the black hole is so much stronger than on the other end that you are stretched like a noodle. A giant black hole is the "better" choice: a small, star-sized one can rip you apart before you even reach the edge.

About the video: the picture is NASA's visualization (the all-sky "camera plunge" movie), reframed for vertical with our own camera moves, timing, text and sound. The approach is sped up; the freeze is our effect to show your friend's view; the 12.8-second countdown runs in real time, but the visualization under it is retimed to fit; the final stretch is our illustration.
