Guess before the end: where do the tears go? 👇

On Earth, gravity pulls your tears down your cheeks. On the Space Station, nothing pulls them down, so they don't fall at all.

Canadian Space Agency astronaut Chris Hadfield tested it in orbit in 2013, using drinking water in place of tears: the water just formed a ball on his eye. If you keep crying, the ball keeps growing until it crosses your nose into your other eye, evaporates, or spreads over your cheek, unless you grab a towel. In his words: your eyes will definitely cry in space, but tears don't fall.

The same thing happens to any loose water up there: in the real photo, NASA astronaut Don Pettit shows a ball of water held together and stuck to research hardware by surface tension (Kibo lab module, 2 March 2025).
