Guess before the end: which planet takes the longest to spin once? 👇

Every planet makes one full spin (one turn on its axis), all on a shared clock.

The giants are fast: Jupiter 9.9 hours, Saturn 10.7 hours, Neptune 16.1 hours, Uranus 17.2 hours.

The rocky planets: Earth 23.9 hours, Mars 24.6 hours (almost the same as Earth), Mercury 58.6 days.

And Venus: 243 days for one spin. That's longer than its whole year: one trip around the Sun takes Venus only 225 days. Venus also spins backward compared to Earth, so on Venus the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east.

About the video: an original animation using real NASA images. Planet sizes are not to scale and the clocks are sped up (the second clock runs faster and faster). The times are rotation periods (one full turn measured against the stars). Sunrise to sunrise is different: about 117 days on Venus and about 176 days on Mercury. Uranus is tipped on its side (about 98°), so its ring is drawn upright.
