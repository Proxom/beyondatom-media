Guess before the end: 15 seconds, 1 minute or 5 minutes? 👇

No spacesuit, no air, just you and the vacuum of space. Breathe out first: holding your breath would be disastrous for your lungs, because the air inside them expands. You would NOT explode (your skin is too strong for that), but you would swell up as the water in your soft tissues starts turning to gas, and the water on your tongue would begin to boil, from the low pressure, not from heat.

You would stay conscious for only about 9 to 11 seconds, with just 5 to 10 seconds to help yourself. In 1965, a NASA test subject in a vacuum chamber, whose suit was leaking, stayed conscious for about 14 seconds. As he passed out, he felt the water on his tongue begin to boil.

The surprise: if you were brought back into air pressure within about 60 to 90 seconds, and you had not held your breath, NASA says you could survive and recover quickly.

About the video: an original computer animation (illustrative spacecraft, Earth and figure). The clock runs in real time up to about 14 seconds, then fast-forwards to 90 seconds. The amount of swelling shown is an illustration, not to scale. Please don't try anything like this: these are emergency limits, and in 1960s decompression tests some animals died after much shorter exposures.
