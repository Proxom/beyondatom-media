Guess before the end: how long would the Sun take, weeks or months? 👇

We fly a jet non-stop at 900 km/h around the equator of every planet, then the Sun. Trip time = equator length (π × diameter) ÷ 900 km/h.

Rocky planets: Mercury 17.0 h (4,879 km across), Mars 23.7 h (6,792 km), Venus 42.3 h (12,104 km), Earth 44.5 h (12,756 km): almost 2 days.

The giants: Neptune 7.2 days (49,528 km), Uranus 7.4 days (51,118 km), Saturn 17.5 days (120,536 km), Jupiter 20.8 days (142,984 km): almost 3 weeks.

The Sun: about 1,391,400 km across (radius 695,700 km), so one lap is about 4.37 million km: 202 days non-stop. More than half a year without ever landing.

About the video: an original animation using real NASA images. It's a thought experiment: a jet can't really fly on Mercury, Mars or the Sun. Distances are measured around each equator (for the giant planets, at the 1-bar level near the cloud tops). Planet sizes are not to scale and the clocks are sped up.
