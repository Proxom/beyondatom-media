Who lands last: the Moon, Europa, Triton or Titania? Guess before the end 👇

Same push-off, four moons, with Earth for comparison. A jump that lifts you 50 cm on Earth would lift you about 3.0 m on our Moon, 3.7 m on Jupiter's moon Europa, 6.3 m on Neptune's moon Triton and 13.4 m on Titania, the biggest moon of Uranus.

With the same take-off speed (about 3.1 m/s), height = v²/(2g) and air time = 2v/g, so both grow as gravity gets weaker. On Earth you are back down in 0.64 s. On the Moon (about 1/6 of Earth's gravity) you stay up for 3.9 s, on Europa 4.8 s and on Triton 8.0 s. Titania's gravity is only about 4% of Earth's, so the same jump would keep you in the air for about 17 seconds.

About the video: an original computer animation (artist's rendering), shown in real time with all five worlds at the same scale. Simplified physics: the same push-off speed everywhere, no air resistance and no spacesuit (a real suit would make the jump lower), and each world's spin is ignored. The landscapes and the planets in the skies are illustrative and not to scale. Surface gravity for Europa (1.32 m/s²), Triton (0.78 m/s²) and Titania (0.37 m/s²) is calculated as g = GM/R² from the mass and mean radius in NASA's satellite fact sheets.
