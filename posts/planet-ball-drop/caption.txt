Guess before the end: which ball lands last? 👇

We drop the same ball from 100 m on all 8 planets, with no air, and time the fall in real time (fall time t = √(2h/g)).

Round 1: Jupiter 2.94 s (gravity 23.1 m/s²), Earth 4.52 s (9.8), Saturn 4.71 s (9.0), Uranus 4.79 s (8.7). Saturn has about 95 times Earth's mass, yet the ball falls slower there than on Earth: that mass is spread over a planet more than 9 times wider (120,536 km vs 12,756 km across), so the pull at its cloud tops is weaker.

Round 2: Neptune 4.26 s (11.0), Venus 4.74 s (8.9), and Mercury and Mars 7.35 s (3.7 each).

Photo finish: Mars has about twice Mercury's mass (0.642 vs 0.330 × 10²⁴ kg), but it is also wider (6,792 km vs 4,879 km across), so the pull at the surface comes out almost the same. With more decimals (mean surface gravity 3.73 m/s² for Mars vs 3.70 for Mercury), Mars pulls very slightly harder and would win by about 3 hundredths of a second.

About the video: an original computer animation. Gravity values are from NASA's Planetary Fact Sheet (at the equator, including the planet's spin; for Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune, which have no solid surface, at the 1-bar pressure level in the atmosphere, shown as "the cloud tops"). No air resistance. Planet and ball sizes are not to scale; the falls run in real time.
