Guess before the end: how deep did NASA's probe get? 👇

Jupiter is a gas giant: it has no true surface to land on. So what happens if you fall in? NASA found out with a robot. On 7 December 1995, the Galileo atmospheric probe slammed into Jupiter's atmosphere at 170,590 km/h (106,000 mph), then slowed down and parachuted through the clouds.

It sent back data for 58 minutes. By then the pressure around it was about 23 times Earth's air pressure at sea level, and temperatures had topped 300 °F (about 150 °C). Then it went silent (NASA's probe page puts the final transmitter failure at 61.4 minutes, about 180 km below its entry point); deeper down, it was eventually vaporized.

How deep did it get? About 156 km (97 miles). Jupiter's equatorial radius is 71,492 km, so that is about 0.22 % of the way to the center: not even a quarter of one percent.

About the video: an edit of real NASA images with our own animation. The falling figure, the probe and its parachute are illustrative silhouettes. Time is compressed (58 minutes of descent shown in about 14 seconds), and the depth counter is illustrative between 0 and 156 km. The red mark for the probe's depth on the last view of Jupiter is drawn about to scale (its thickness is exaggerated about 2× for visibility).
